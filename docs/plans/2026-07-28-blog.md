**Goal:** Ship `/blog` and `/blog/[slug]` in both locales, plus one substantial launch
article — `how-to-read-tarot`, authored twice rather than translated once — as static,
session-less, database-free, CDN-cacheable pages carrying `Blog` / `BlogPosting` /
`BreadcrumbList` structured data and reciprocal `hreflang`.

**Architecture:** Prose is committed TypeScript data, never MDX and never the i18n
catalog (S-D6). One typed block union in `src/content/types.ts` — **written by S4,
imported by S6** — is rendered by one server component. The page is locale-agnostic code
reading `await getT()`; the `/en/` twin arrives through S2's middleware rewrite, so there
is one route tree and no `[locale]` segment. Nothing on either route reads a cookie, a
session, a database or a model. The share control is `navigator.share()` on the page's own
canonical URL (S-D8), never `/api/share`.

**Tech Stack:** Next 15 App Router (server components), the existing W6 i18n pipe
(`getLocale` / `getT` / `x-jmt-locale`), `src/theme/tokens.css` custom properties, Vitest.
**No new dependency:** no MDX, no `gray-matter`, no `remark`, no `reading-time`, no schema
library.

## What is in scope, and the five rules that shape all of it

1. **No model call, at build time or request time** (S-D7). The article is authored during
   implementation, read by a human, and committed as source. There is no `/api/` route in
   this workstream.
2. **No cookie, no session, no database** (S-D10, S-D14). A `Set-Cookie` makes the response
   uncacheable at the edge, and these are the pages whose TTFB a crawler measures. Nothing
   here can 500 on a database outage because nothing here touches a database.
3. **No `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` anywhere**, including the JSON-LD script. §D2 carries the
   escape that makes that possible without one.
4. **The prose is data, not markup.** A typed block union rendered by one component.
   **This also buys something `/terms` had to pay for:** `legal.test.ts`'s
   JSX-whitespace trap — *"JSX strips the leading whitespace of a text node that spans more
   than one line"*, a bug that shipped three times in one afternoon — cannot occur in a
   content module, because the prose is string data rather than JSX text nodes. The
   analogous hazard moves to span adjacency inside a paragraph, and Task 5 puts a test on
   it.
5. **The page is complete and readable with JavaScript disabled.** Every link is a real
   `<a href>`. The only client components are the share button, which renders nothing until
   it has mounted, and three analytics mounts that render nothing at all.

### Why the prose is NOT in the i18n catalog (S-D6)

I9: the client is shipped exactly **one** catalog, as JSON, from the server. `locales/id.ts`
is 843 lines today and holds 118 keys of chrome. The launch article is ~1,500 words per
locale; putting it in the catalog would ship every word of it to **every visitor of every
page, including the draw screen**, in the locale they are not reading as well as the one
they are.

So: article prose, `title` and `description` live in `src/content/blog/*.{id,en}.ts`,
imported only by the page that renders them, on the server. **Only chrome keys go in the
catalog** — `blog.index.title`, `blog.readMore`, `blog.published`, and the six others in
§D4 — and they go in `id.ts` first, so a missing English string is a red `npm run typecheck`
(TS2739) rather than an Indonesian sentence inside the English app (I2, I3).

`<title>` and `<meta name="description">` are the highest-leverage strings in this
workstream and they live on `BlogDoc` for exactly the same reason: they are per-article
prose, not chrome, and a `meta.description` key per article per locale would be four
catalog values today and ninety when S4's lore pages want the same thing.

### Structured data, dates, and who the author is (S-D16)

- `/blog` emits **`Blog`** (with a `blogPost` array of the articles' `BlogPosting` `@id`s)
  plus **`BreadcrumbList`**.
- `/blog/[slug]` emits **`BlogPosting`** plus **`BreadcrumbList`**.
- No `SearchAction`, no `FAQPage` — S-D16 forbids both and the second buys nothing since
  Google restricted FAQ rich results in August 2023.

**The author is the organisation, not a person and not a reader.** Three reasons, and the
second is the binding one:

1. There is no human author to name. Inventing a byline is fabricating an identity on a
   page whose whole purpose is to be trusted by a stranger.
2. **Bylining it to Thessaly, Margaret or Adrian would contradict `/terms` clause 4.4**,
   which says in both locales that the three readers *"adalah persona, bukan orang. Mereka
   tidak punya kualifikasi apa pun"*. Attributing an instructional article to a character
   the terms describe as unqualified is a claim the terms deny.
3. S1 emits exactly one `Organization` node with a stable `@id`. Referencing it —
   `author: { '@id': ORG_ID }` — makes the identity consistent **by construction** rather
   than by two files agreeing about a name.

**`datePublished` and `dateModified` are committed literals, and here is what happens when
the content is edited.** There is no honest automatic source: a filesystem mtime is a
checkout artefact on Vercel, `git log` is not available at request time, and either one
moves on a whitespace change. So both dates are hand-written `YYYY-MM-DD` strings in
`src/content/blog/index.ts`, per locale for `dateModified` (the two locales are two URLs and
two `BlogPosting` nodes; claiming the English changed because the Indonesian did is a small
lie in structured data).

Hand-written dates rot. The mechanism that stops it: the registry also carries a
**`bodyHash`** per locale, and Task 5's test recomputes it. **Edit the prose, the hash test
fails, and its message tells you to bump `dateModified` and paste the new hash.** That is
one line of bookkeeping per edit and it is the only thing that makes `dateModified` a fact
rather than decoration.

import type { Block, Inline } from './types';

/**
 * Authoring sugar for `src/content/**`. **NOTHING HERE IS PROSE.**
 *
 * WHY IT EXISTS. Forty-six committed documents typed as raw object literals are
 * unreviewable in a diff — the same argument `src/components/Legal.tsx` made for
 * `<P>` over `<p className={styles.p}>`. These nine functions are the whole
 * vocabulary an author needs and they are checked by `types.contract.test.ts`
 * against S4's union rather than by restating it.
 *
 * NO `server-only` MARKER and no imports beyond the type: `blog.content.test.ts`
 * imports this, and the marker throws outside a Next server bundle. It is still
 * fenced from client components by `clientBoundary.test.ts`'s `src/content/**`
 * rule, alongside the prose it builds.
 *
 * OFFERED TO S4. If S4's plan has its own builders, reconciliation keeps one set;
 * the names are the interface and they are deliberately short because they appear
 * a thousand times.
 *
 * **SPANS CARRY THEIR OWN SPACES.** `para(s('Lihat '), link('/gallery', 'galeri'))`
 * renders `Lihat galeri`; drop the trailing space in `s()` and it renders
 * `Lihatgaleri`. React does not insert whitespace between adjacent children, so
 * this is the block union's version of the JSX-whitespace bug `legal.test.ts`
 * describes — and `blog.content.test.ts` has a test for it, for the same reason
 * that one exists: the bug shipped three times in one afternoon.
 */

export const s = (text: string): Inline => ({ kind: 'text', text });
export const em = (text: string): Inline => ({ kind: 'em', text });
export const strong = (text: string): Inline => ({ kind: 'strong', text });

/**
 * An INTERNAL link, by bare path. `/arcana/the-moon`, never `/en/arcana/the-moon`:
 * `Blocks.tsx` applies S2's `localePath()`, so one document serves both prefixes and
 * nothing here duplicates the locale scheme.
 */
export const link = (path: string, text: string): Inline => ({ kind: 'link', path, text });

export const h2 = (id: string, text: string): Block => ({ kind: 'heading', level: 2, id, text });
export const h3 = (id: string, text: string): Block => ({ kind: 'heading', level: 3, id, text });

export const para = (...text: Inline[]): Block => ({ kind: 'paragraph', text });
export const bullets = (...items: Inline[][]): Block => ({ kind: 'list', ordered: false, items });
export const steps = (...items: Inline[][]): Block => ({ kind: 'list', ordered: true, items });
export const quote = (...text: Inline[]): Block => ({ kind: 'quote', text });

/** An aside that must not be skimmed past. `Legal.tsx`'s `Callout`, as data. */
export const note = (...text: Inline[]): Block => ({ kind: 'callout', text });

/**
 * One card, beside the paragraph that discusses it. `urlSlug` is S-D4's hyphenated
 * English name (`the-moon`), resolved by S4's `cardByUrlSlug` at render time — so a
 * typo is a 404 in a test rather than a broken image in production.
 */
export const cardRef = (urlSlug: string, caption: string): Block => ({
  kind: 'cardRef',
  urlSlug,
  caption,
});
```

import type { Block, Inline } from '@/content/types';

/**
 * Questions you can ask a content document without rendering it.
 *
 * PURE AND LEAF. No React, no `next/*`, no env, no `server-only`. The copy lint, the
 * chrome (`blog.readingTime`) and the analytics classifier all need answers about a
 * document, and none of them should have to mount a component to get one.
 *
 * WHY `plainText` MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS. It is the input to §11.4's copy lint —
 * the Malay grep and the therapy/diagnosis list, which until v0.4.0 ran only against
 * *generated* readings and therefore never saw a word of the permanent copy a stranger
 * reads first. If this function silently skipped a block variant, that variant would
 * be un-linted, so `doc.test.ts` names every variant explicitly.
 */

const inlineText = (spans: readonly Inline[]): string => spans.map((x) => x.text).join('');

/**
 * Every word a reader sees, blocks separated by `\n`.
 *
 * **SPANS ARE JOINED WITH NOTHING AND BLOCKS WITH A NEWLINE**, and both halves are
 * deliberate. Joining spans with a space would hide the adjacency bug
 * `blog.content.test.ts` exists to catch; joining blocks with nothing would let the
 * last word of one paragraph and the first of the next form a word that is in neither,
 * which is exactly how a word-boundary grep produces a false positive nobody can find.
 */
export function plainText(blocks: readonly Block[]): string {
  const out: string[] = [];
  for (const block of blocks) {
    switch (block.kind) {
      case 'heading':
        out.push(block.text);
        break;
      case 'paragraph':
      case 'quote':
      case 'callout':
        out.push(inlineText(block.text));
        break;
      case 'list':
        for (const item of block.items) out.push(inlineText(item));
        break;
      case 'cardRef':
        out.push(block.caption);
        break;
    }
  }
  return out.join('\n');
}

export function wordCount(blocks: readonly Block[]): number {
  const words = plainText(blocks).trim().split(/\s+/);
  return words.length === 1 && words[0] === '' ? 0 : words.length;
}

/**
 * A rounded estimate at 200 words per minute, floored at one.
 *
 * 200 wpm is the conventional silent-reading figure and it is close enough for both
 * languages at this length; nobody has measured Indonesian prose here and the plan does
 * not pretend otherwise. The floor exists because "0 menit baca" reads as a bug.
 */
export function readingMinutes(words: number): number {
  return Math.max(1, Math.round(words / 200));
}

/** Heading ids at one level, in document order. Feeds the in-page nav and the anchor test. */
export function headingIds(blocks: readonly Block[], level: 2 | 3): string[] {
  return blocks.flatMap((b) => (b.kind === 'heading' && b.level === level ? [b.id] : []));
}

/**
 * What a link points at, as a CLOSED set.
 *
 * **THIS EXISTS SO THE ANALYTICS EVENT NEVER CARRIES AN HREF.** `events.ts` rule 1 is
 * no free text and rule 2 is no unbounded cardinality; a path is both. Five tokens
 * answer the only question worth asking of an internal-link click — is the article
 * feeding the lore pages, the gallery, itself, or the app.
 *
 * MATCHED AS A SEGMENT, NEVER AS A PREFIX. `/galleryish` is not the gallery, for the
 * same reason `isPublic()` is a function rather than a regex.
 */
export type LinkKind = 'arcana' | 'gallery' | 'blog' | 'app' | 'anchor';

export function linkKind(path: string): LinkKind {
  if (path.startsWith('#')) return 'anchor';
  if (path === '/gallery') return 'gallery';
  if (path === '/blog' || path.startsWith('/blog/')) return 'blog';
  if (path.startsWith('/arcana/')) return 'arcana';
  return 'app';
}

/**

 *

 * typed union rather than a string of HTML: the CSP is `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'`
 * in report-only and the goal is to tighten it, not to acquire a permanent reason it never
 * can be (roadmap §5 rule 3, §10).
 *
 * **INTERNAL LINKS ARRIVE AS BARE PATHS AND ARE PREFIXED HERE**, by S2's `localePath()`.
 * A content module that hard-coded `/en/...` would duplicate the prefix scheme and put an
 * English href in the Indonesian document. An in-page anchor is left alone: `localePath`
 * on `#reversals` yields `/en/#reversals`, which navigates away from the article.
 *
 * `<a>` RATHER THAN `<Link>`, DELIBERATELY. These pages are CDN-cached and session-less;
 * a client-side router transition into another statically cacheable document buys nothing
 * and `<Link>` prefetches 22 lore pages from an article that links to four of them. A real
 * `href` is also what makes the page work with JavaScript disabled, which is a hard
 * requirement here.
 *
 * `data-content-link` CARRIES A CLOSED CLASSIFICATION, NEVER THE HREF. One delegated
 * click listener (`ContentLinks`) reads it, so no link becomes a client component and no
 * analytics prop carries free text (`events.ts` rules 1 and 2).
 */

function Spans({ spans, locale }: { spans: readonly Inline[]; locale: Locale }) {
  return (
    <>
      {spans.map((span, i) => {
        switch (span.kind) {
          case 'text':
            return span.text;
          case 'em':
            return <em key={i}>{span.text}</em>;
          case 'strong':
            return <strong key={i}>{span.text}</strong>;
          case 'link': {
            const href = span.path.startsWith('#') ? span.path : localePath(span.path, locale);
            return (
              <a key={i} href={href} data-content-link={linkKind(span.path)}>
                {span.text}
              </a>
            );
          }
        }
      })}
    </>
  );
}

export function Blocks({ blocks, locale }: { blocks: readonly Block[]; locale: Locale }) {
  return (
    <>
      {blocks.map((block, i) => {
        switch (block.kind) {
          case 'heading':
            return block.level === 2 ? (
              <h2 key={i} id={block.id} className={styles.h2}>
                {block.text}
              </h2>
            ) : (
              <h3 key={i} id={block.id} className={styles.h3}>
                {block.text}
              </h3>
            );

          case 'paragraph':
            return (
              <p key={i} className={styles.p}>
                <Spans spans={block.text} locale={locale} />
              </p>
            );

          case 'list': {
            const items = block.items.map((item, j) => (
              <li key={j} className={styles.li}>
                <Spans spans={item} locale={locale} />
              </li>
            ));
            return block.ordered ? (
              <ol key={i} className={styles.ol}>
                {items}
              </ol>
            ) : (
              <ul key={i} className={styles.ul}>
                {items}
              </ul>
            );
          }

          case 'quote':
            return (
              <blockquote key={i} className={styles.quote}>
                <p className={styles.p}>
                  <Spans spans={block.text} locale={locale} />
                </p>
              </blockquote>
            );

          case 'callout':
            return (
              <aside key={i} className={styles.callout}>
                <p className={styles.p}>
                  <Spans spans={block.text} locale={locale} />
                </p>
              </aside>
            );

          case 'cardRef': {
            const card = cardByUrlSlug(block.urlSlug);
            /*
             * A typo in a slug renders NOTHING rather than throwing. A public page must
             * not 500 over a caption, and `blocks.test.ts` pins the behaviour so the
             * silence is a decision rather than an accident. The real guard is S4's
             * §3.2 slug assertion, which fails in a test.
             */
            if (!card) return null;
            return (
              <figure key={i} className={styles.card}>
                <a href={localePath(`/arcana/${block.urlSlug}`, locale)} data-content-link="arcana">
                  {/* A plain <img>: `cardThumb` appends `?v=` and `next/image` refuses a
                      local src with a query string unless `images.localPatterns` is set —
                      the constraint `AccountCard` records. `CardFace` does the same. */}
                  <img
                    className={styles.cardArt}
                    src={cardThumb(card.slug)}
                    alt={card.name}
                    width={240}
                    height={360}
                    loading="lazy"
                    decoding="async"
                  />
                  <span className={styles.cardName}>{card.name}</span>
                </a>
                <figcaption className={styles.caption}>{block.caption}</figcaption>
              </figure>
            );
          }
        }
      })}
    </>
  );
}
```

container-driven layout and is what the roadmap's §8.1 and §11.1 both name.

**Do I reuse `Legal.module.css`? No, and here is the reason written down.** What is reused

1. `Legal.module.css` is built around a numbered-clause rhythm: `.number` in tabular

   numbers and a left rule on every `h3` would read as a quote.
2. An article needs furniture the legal documents do not have — a hero plate, a byline and

3. **The legal documents are a contract.** Sharing a stylesheet means a typographic
   improvement for the blog silently restyles `/terms`, which is the file `legal.test.ts`
   exists to keep stable.

**No new design token, and that is not a shortcut.** The body is `--fs-reading` /
`--lh-reading` — 19px on 28px, the same values a *reading* renders at. That is a coherence
argument: an article about how to read tarot set in the same type as a reading looks like it
came from the same house. `tokens.ts` is untouched, so `tokens.css` needs no mirror edit.

  const WIDTHS = [320, 360, 390];
  const PAGES = [
    ['id', '/blog/how-to-read-tarot'],
    ['en', '/en/blog/how-to-read-tarot'],
  ];
  const tbody = document.querySelector('#out tbody');

  for (const w of WIDTHS) {
    for (const [locale, path] of PAGES) {
      const frame = document.createElement('iframe');
      frame.width = w;
      frame.height = 900;
      frame.src = path;
      document.getElementById('frames').append(frame);

        const doc = frame.contentDocument;
        const body = doc.querySelector('[data-article-body]');
        if (!body) return;

        // A `ch`-calibrated probe: measure one character of the ACTUAL body font.
        const probe = doc.createElement('span');

          'position:absolute;visibility:hidden;white-space:pre;' +
          getComputedStyle(body.querySelector('p')).font;

        const overflowing = [...body.querySelectorAll('p,h2,h3,li,figure,aside,blockquote')]
          .filter((el) => el.scrollWidth > el.clientWidth + 1)
          .map((el) => el.tagName.toLowerCase());

          `<td>${w}<td>${locale}<td>${Math.round(px)}<td>${chars}` +

      }

      setCopied(true);
      window.setTimeout(() => setCopied(false), 2000);
      track('content.shared', { surface, method: 'clipboard' });
    } catch {
      track('content.shared', { surface, method: 'failed' });
    }

'use client';

import { useEffect, useRef } from 'react';
import { track } from '@/lib/analytics/track.client';

/**
 * How far down a long article a reader actually got.
 *
 * CHEAP AND HONEST, which is the bar the plan set for including it at all. Four
 * `IntersectionObserver` sentinels, at most four events per reader, each fired once and
 * then unobserved — and the client tracker already batches with a 2s debounce, so the
 * usual outcome is ONE request carrying the view and every threshold reached.
 *
 * NOT A SCROLL LISTENER. A `scroll` handler on a 2,400-word article fires hundreds of
 * times and would be the only thing on this page capable of costing a frame.
 *
 * `depth` IS A CLOSED SET OF FOUR NUMBERS (`events.ts` rule 4: one event with props, not
 * four events). It measures depth REACHED, not time spent, and it does not claim the
 * article was read — a fact stated here because a `depth: 100` will eventually be quoted
 * as a completion rate.
 */
const THRESHOLDS = [25, 50, 75, 100] as const;

export function ScrollDepth({ slug, locale }: { slug: string; locale: string }) {
  const seen = useRef(new Set<number>());

  useEffect(() => {
    const nodes = THRESHOLDS.map((depth) =>
      document.querySelector<HTMLElement>(`[data-depth="${depth}"]`),
    );
    if (nodes.every((n) => n === null)) return;

    const observer = new IntersectionObserver(
      (entries) => {
        for (const entry of entries) {
          if (!entry.isIntersecting) continue;
          const depth = Number((entry.target as HTMLElement).dataset.depth);
          if (seen.current.has(depth)) continue;
          seen.current.add(depth);
          observer.unobserve(entry.target);
          track('content.scrolled', { slug, locale, depth });
        }
      },
      { rootMargin: '0px 0px -10% 0px' },
    );

    for (const node of nodes) if (node) observer.observe(node);
    return () => observer.disconnect();
  }, [slug, locale]);

  return null;
}

 *      the locale (§4.1) — S2's middleware sets `x-jmt-locale` from the path
 *      unconditionally — because a page whose language depends on the visitor's jar
 *      cannot be cached at the edge and cannot be canonicalised.
 *   3. **Nothing here touches the database.** So there is no version of a database
 *      outage that makes this page 500 (roadmap §10), and no fourth route joining the
 *      three that already return 500 instead of 204.
 *   4. **Nothing here calls a model** (S-D7). The article is committed source.
 *
 * `dynamicParams = false` + `generateStaticParams` CLOSE THE SLUG SPACE, and that is what
 * they are for here — **they do not make the page static.** The root layout awaits
 * `getLocale()` for `<html lang>` and `## Localization` rule 5 forbids "fixing" that, so
 * every route in this app builds as `ƒ`. Speed comes from S1's `s-maxage` (S-D10), which
 * gets a crawler the same TTFB at none of the cost of a second root layout.
 *
 * `maxDuration` IS DECLARED even though nothing here is slow. `POST /api/locale` was the
 * only database-writing route declaring neither `runtime` nor `maxDuration` and Hobby's
 * default ten seconds truncated it cold. This page has no database and no model, so ten
 * seconds would very likely be enough — "very likely" is exactly the reasoning that trap
 * punished, and the declaration costs nothing.
 */

### Task 15: The signed-out crawl, and the build

**Files:** none. This is the acceptance test.

**Step 1: The `curl` sweep, with no cookie jar**

Roadmap §11.2 is the single check that matters and `curl -i` is the right instrument for
most of this release. Against `npm run dev` on 3001 and then against a Vercel preview:

```sh
BASE=http://localhost:3001
for p in /blog /en/blog /blog/how-to-read-tarot /en/blog/how-to-read-tarot; do
  echo "--- $p"
  curl -sS -D - -o /dev/null "$BASE$p"
done
```

Every one must be:
- **`200`** — a 302 to `/login` means S1's `isPublic()` does not know this path, and a
  302 anywhere in that list is the release failing at its only purpose.
- **carrying no `Set-Cookie`** — S-D10. A single `Set-Cookie` makes the response
  uncacheable at the edge on the pages whose TTFB a crawler measures.
- **carrying no `x-robots-tag`** — S-D12. `/s/:path*`'s `noindex, nofollow, noarchive`
  must not have spread; if it appears here, a new `headers()` entry matched too broadly.

**Step 2: The locale check that cannot be done any other way**

```sh
# THE URL WINS (§4.1). An English cookie must not change a byte of the bare path.
curl -sS -H 'Cookie: jmt_locale=en' "$BASE/blog/how-to-read-tarot" | grep -c 'Cara Membaca'
curl -sS -H 'Accept-Language: en-GB,en;q=0.9' "$BASE/blog/how-to-read-tarot" | grep -c 'Cara Membaca'
curl -sS "$BASE/en/blog/how-to-read-tarot" | grep -c 'How to Read Tarot'
```

Expected: `1`, `1`, `1`. **If either of the first two returns `0`, the bug is S2's** — the
middleware is falling through to the cookie on a content route instead of setting
`x-jmt-locale` from the URL — and it is the failure mode S-D2 warns about, which *"presents
as a page that is occasionally in the wrong language"* and would additionally poison the
CDN.

**Step 3: The metadata, read off the wire**

escapes `&` in a text child, and the symptom of getting it wrong is a *valid* JSON document

every title carrying an ampersand.

```tsx
/**
 * A JSON-LD block, with **NO `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`** (roadmap §5 rule 3, §10).
 *
 * **THE OBVIOUS VERSION IS SILENTLY WRONG AND NO VALIDATOR CATCHES IT.** React escapes
 * `&`, `<` and `>` in a text child, and a `<script>` element is raw text to the HTML
 * parser — so `<script>{JSON.stringify(x)}</script>` emits `Syarat &amp; Ketentuan`
 * *inside the JSON string value*. The document parses, every validator passes, and Google
 * reads `&amp;`. That is why every tutorial reaches for `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`.
 *
 * **THE FIX IS TO PRE-ESCAPE SO REACT'S ESCAPER HAS NOTHING TO DO.** Replacing `& < >`
 * with their `\uXXXX` JSON escapes leaves raw text containing none of those three
 * characters, so React passes it through unchanged and `JSON.parse` restores the
 * originals. It also closes the `</script>` injection that `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`
 * needs a separate `.replace(/</g, '\\u003c')` for.
 *
 * ASSERT IT, DO NOT TRUST IT: a test must render a node containing `&` and `<`, pull the
 * script's text out of the markup, `JSON.parse` it, and compare to the original object.
 */
export function jsonLdText(node: unknown): string {
  return JSON.stringify(node).replace(
    /[&<>]/g,
    (c) => `\\u00${c.charCodeAt(0).toString(16).padStart(2, '0')}`,
  );
}

export function JsonLd({ node }: { node: object }) {
  return <script type="application/ld+json">{jsonLdText(node)}</script>;
}
```

## Flags

1. **ONE ARTICLE OR TWO — Miftah's call, and roadmap §13 leaves it open.** This plan ships
   **one**, because §7 (S6) says one and the roadmap outranks the plan. **The reason it is
   still a flag is that Jodith's request is not fully answered by an anchor.** A footer link
   labelled *"what is tarot"* that lands two-thirds of the way into a 2,400-word how-to is a
   compromise, not a design; a dedicated `apa-itu-tarot` / `what-tarot-is` piece would be a
   page in its own right, would target `apa itu tarot` as a query the how-to article
   competes with itself for, and would double the blog's indexed surface. **Cost: two more
   documents at ~1,200 words each, and the release lands later.** Everything in this plan is
   built so a second article is purely additive — one registry entry, two modules, no code
   change, and D8's three footer links move to it. **Recommendation: ship one, and write the
   second the week after v0.4.0 lands**, because the card pages are the release's structural
   bet and a blog that grows monthly beats a blog that shipped twice.

2. **`og:image` is 2:3 card art on a page whose previews want ~1.91:1.** Every messenger
   that sees the link caches that image. S-D9 forbids new derived art in this workstream and
   S5 owns the pipeline, so the options are: accept the portrait crop most platforms will
   apply; inherit a site-level default from S1's `metadataBase`/`openGraph` (**preferred, and
   D2 asks for it**); or add one 1200×630 blog card to S5's scope. **Not decided.**

3. **`bodyHash` is manual bookkeeping and somebody will want to delete it.** Editing the

   it is the only thing making `BlogPosting.dateModified` a fact — but it is friction on
   every copy edit, and the honest alternative is dropping `dateModified` from the
   structured data entirely rather than emitting a date nobody maintains. **Keep it unless
   the friction is measured to be worse than the claim is worth.**

   arithmetic rather than a defect.** 288px of content at ~8.4px per character in Cormorant
